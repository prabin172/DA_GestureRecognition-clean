## 1. Introduction

Wearable inertial measurement units (IMUs) are the practical substrate for gesture and activity recognition in the wild: they are cheap, privacy-preserving relative to cameras, and deployable on a wrist or garment [Ordonez & Roggen 2016; Cai & Hong 2025]. Yet the wearable modality is chronically starved of labeled data. Annotating body-worn sensor streams is expensive and error-prone, and every new user, sensor placement, or gesture vocabulary reopens the labeling problem [Haresamudram et al. 2024; Xu et al. 2021]. Vision- and motion-capture-based skeleton datasets, by contrast, are large, richly labeled, and semantically close to the underlying body motion — NTU RGB+D alone provides ~43k clips across 120 action classes. This asymmetry motivates a natural strategy: **pretrain a motion representation on abundant skeleton data and transfer it to the data-scarce wearable domain.**

The strategy works — but *how* it should be trained is unsettled. A representation can be pretrained with a purely discriminative (supervised classification) objective, a reconstruction (masked/auto-encoding) objective, a contrastive objective, or a hybrid of these. These objectives shape the geometry of the learned feature space in very different ways, and it is not obvious which geometry survives the jump from a vision-captured skeleton to a body-worn inertial sensor. This paper asks a deliberately narrow, controlled question: **as the skeleton→wearable domain gap grows, how does the choice of pretraining objective change what transfers — and when does a well-motivated objective actively hurt?**

## 2. Background

**Cross-modal transfer for HAR.** Human activity recognition spans many modalities — RGB, skeleton, depth, audio, WiFi, and inertial — each with distinct strengths [Sun et al. 2020]. Skeleton/pose is attractive as a *source* modality because it captures modality-agnostic body kinematics that can, in principle, be re-expressed as inertial signals. Prior work exploits this in three broad ways: (i) **direct representation transfer**, where a skeleton-pretrained encoder is reused or aligned for IMU [Moya Rueda & Fink 2021; Li & Gupta 2025]; (ii) **signal simulation**, where skeleton/video is used to synthesize virtual IMU for augmentation [Kwon et al. 2020; Lämsä et al. 2022; Hao et al. 2024]; and (iii) **joint multimodal spaces**, where skeleton, video, IMU, and text are embedded together [Moon et al. 2022; Zhang et al. 2024b; Matsuishi & Okita 2025].

**Pretraining objectives and the domain gap.** The wearable-sensing literature has, over the last five years, systematically drifted from supervised to self-supervised and hybrid objectives as the deployment gap widens [Haresamudram et al. 2020; Xu et al. 2021; Cai & Hong 2026]. Within-IMU benchmarks now report that **hybrid reconstruction+contrastive pretraining generalizes best under distribution shift**, outperforming purely discriminative or purely contrastive alternatives [Cai & Hong 2026 (BenchHAR); Das & Malekzadeh 2025 (PRIMUS)]. The intuition is that discriminative objectives compress away exactly the fine temporal and orientation structure that a downstream cross-modal task needs, whereas generative/structure-preserving objectives retain it.

**Deployment-realistic protocols.** Because labels are scarce, the operationally relevant setting is not full-data training but **subject-held-out few-shot calibration** (a handful of labeled repetitions from a new user) and **novel-class onboarding** (adding a gesture with a few shots) [Thukral & Ploetz 2023; Chowdhury et al. 2025]. These protocols, rather than large-N in-distribution accuracy, are where objective choice is most likely to matter.

## 3. Previous work

**Supervised skeleton/pose → IMU transfer (low–moderate gap).** Moya Rueda & Fink [2021] pretrain temporal CNNs on human-pose sequences with supervised HAR objectives and finetune on IMU, reporting gains on three benchmarks; Awasthi et al. [2022] extend this to multiple video-pose sources. Both demonstrate that *discriminative supervised skeleton pretraining helps when the modality gap is moderate and class taxonomies align* — but neither examines out-of-distribution or novel-class generalization, nor measures negative transfer.

**Cross-modal knowledge distillation.** PSKD [Ni et al. 2022] progressively distills skeleton and accelerometer teachers into an IMU student; TAS [Qiao et al. 2026] adds dense temporal contrastive learning, spatial-relationship learning, and attention transfer, explicitly arguing that vanilla supervised teacher–student distillation is *insufficient* across the large skeleton→single-IMU gap. These works bridge the gap architecturally and adopt hybrid/contrastive components by design — they do not contrast objectives head-to-head.

**Reconstruction-anchored and simulation approaches.** SKELAR [Li & Gupta 2025] pretrains a skeleton encoder via self-supervised coarse joint-angle reconstruction ("core motion knowledge without context-specific details") and adapts to heterogeneous signals via attention matching; Zolfaghari et al. [2024] jointly optimize a pose→IMU reconstruction loss and a HAR classification loss. The simulation line — IMUTube [Kwon et al. 2020], Video2IMU [Lämsä et al. 2022], CROMOSim [Hao et al. 2022] — uses reconstruction/regression to synthesize IMU for augmentation. All adopt reconstruction *a priori*; none isolates the benefit of reconstruction versus supervision.

**Multimodal contrastive and foundation models.** IMU2CLIP [Moon et al. 2022], UniMTS [Zhang et al. 2024b], and AURA-MFM [Matsuishi & Okita 2025] align IMU with text/video/mocap via contrastive objectives and achieve strong zero-/few-shot and novel-class performance, consistent with the view that supervised objectives shape latent spaces too narrowly around a fixed class taxonomy. Cross-modal IMU–video SSL [Cheshmi et al. 2025] and masked video+IMU autoencoding [Zhang & Sato 2024] similarly show reconstruction/contrastive robustness to shifted or missing modalities.

**Systematic objective comparisons — but only within IMU.** The only rigorous, objective-vs-gap comparisons with robust conclusions live in **IMU-only** SSL benchmarks: BenchHAR [Cai & Hong 2026] evaluates eight SSL methods across cross-dataset OOD shifts and finds hybrid recon+contrastive best; PRIMUS [Das & Malekzadeh 2025] reports up to 15% few-shot gains from a composite objective on OOD tasks. These provide the empirical backbone for "richer objectives are more robust under larger gaps" — but with a *small*, within-modality gap.

## 4. The gap

Bringing these strands together exposes a precise hole. Direct skeleton/vision→IMU transfer works [Moya Rueda & Fink 2021; Awasthi et al. 2022; Ni et al. 2022] but *assumes* richer objectives help; multimodal foundation models [Moon et al. 2022; Zhang et al. 2024b; Matsuishi & Okita 2025] adopt contrastive objectives from the outset; and the only controlled objective-vs-gap studies [Cai & Hong 2026; Das & Malekzadeh 2025] never use skeleton/vision as the source modality. **No existing work systematically varies both the pretraining objective and the skeleton→IMU domain gap, and none explicitly demonstrates or explains *negative transfer* of supervised skeleton pretraining under large gaps.** The field has "voted with its feet" — abandoning pure supervised classification for reconstruction/hybrid objectives as gaps grow — but the *when* and *why* remain motivation rather than measured evidence. This is precisely the gap our study fills, under deployment-realistic few-shot and novel-class protocols, and with a representation-level mechanism rather than an assertion.

## 5. Our contributions

We present the first controlled characterization of how pretraining-objective utility depends on the skeleton→wearable domain gap, transferring an NTU RGB+D-pretrained Transformer encoder to a small Xsens inertial gesture dataset (22 gestures, 5 subjects) over a shared Local Relative Quaternion representation. Specifically:

1. **Objective utility is conditional on the gap.** Within-domain, objective choice drives a ~45 pp accuracy spread (NTU linear probe: supervised 59.6%, hybrid Sup+MAE 59.2%, reconstruction-only MAE 22.6%, scratch 14.8%). Across the skeleton→IMU gap the same choice compresses to ~7 pp at one-shot calibration. The effect is a property of the *gap*, not of the objective.

2. **A seed-stable negative-transfer case that inverts the field's assumption — at the middle-sized gap.** The literature treats reconstruction/self-supervised objectives as the *safe* choice under large gaps. In our NTU→Xsens setting (cross-device, but skeleton-like target — see the five-setting map in contribution 6) the opposite holds: **pure masked reconstruction (MAE) transfers *below* random init at every shot count** (79.5% vs 82.3% at k=1), significant under a clip-level McNemar test at every k (net −334/−217/−92 clips at k=0/1/3, p<.001, pooled over three seeds). The hybrid (reconstruction+supervision) is the robust best, and the purely-supervised prior — assumed to over-specialize — is neutral-to-positive on accuracy and the *best-calibrated* of all. "Richer objectives are safer" is too coarse even at this one gap size: a reconstruction signal with no discriminative component is the one that hurts. This is not the general rule, though — it is one leg of a broader pattern in which *which* prior is safe and which is harmful both shift with gap width and target strength (contribution 6).

3. **A representation-level mechanism.** A small domain gap is *necessary but not sufficient*: feature-distribution distance (MMD) does not track transfer. Layer-wise CKA between source and target features orders the objectives cleanly and monotonically with depth — the supervised encoder aligns 3–5× better than the rest, the reconstruction encoder barely above random — yet alignment alone does not predict accuracy, showing that the *discriminative content* of a representation, not just its cross-domain similarity, governs transfer.

4. **The prior's value is calibration and data-efficiency, not headline accuracy.** Across the gap the accuracy spread compresses to a few points, but the pretrained prior still (i) **calibrates better** (lowest temperature-scaled ECE at every shot count), (ii) **converges 3–4 epochs faster** during calibration (p≤.012), and (iii) **matters most when enrolled data is scarcest** — a subject-count scaling sweep shows the prior's benefit peaks with 0–1 fine-tuning subjects (+27 pp at k=3) and washes to ≈1 pp once four subjects are available. All under subject-held-out few-shot LOSO calibration and novel-class (OOV) onboarding.

5. **From recognition to control.** We close the human–machine-systems loop with an abstract event-driven controller driven by the *actual* recognizer's posterior stream, showing that these compressed accuracy differences **compound** into large task-level ones (mae's negative transfer drops task success to 0.75 vs 0.97 for the hybrid at k=1) and that the best-calibrated prior yields the safest confidence-threshold operating points — turning "a few accuracy points" into control-reliability consequences.

6. **A five-setting map: the same prior is hero and villain, as a function of gap width and target strength (Results R6e).** On two independently collected public datasets with a small, same-modality (skeleton→skeleton) gap — CZU-MHAD and UTD-MHAD — the pure-supervised prior supLP120 is the best-in-class transfer, pooled over 3 seeds: **+7.2 pp zero-shot on CZU** (p=.0004) and **+7.4 pp on UTD** (p<.0001), with the reconstruction objective (mae) positive-or-neutral rather than harmful on both. At the middle-sized NTU→Xsens gap the ranking flips: the hybrid objective wins and pure reconstruction is the one that hurts (contribution 2). Widening the gap further to skeleton→wearable-IMU on the *same* CZU platform inverts the supervised prior's standing twice over. With an impoverished, orientation-only target encoding, supLP120 becomes the **worst** prior — below scratch at every k (p≈.04–.06) — while adding reconstruction rescues transfer (supMAE > supLP120 in 36/44 folds, p<.0001). Giving the target its full raw signal (a dual-branch recognizer whose from-scratch arm already matches a hand-crafted CRC baseline) removes even that rescue: no prior beats scratch, and supLP120 remains worst, now at p<.0001. Five independently pooled, multi-seed legs across two public datasets and our own middle-gap setting turn the negative-transfer thesis from a single-pair anomaly into a demonstrated design rule: a transferred prior's value is set jointly by the size of the modality gap and the poverty of the target's own representation — no single objective is unconditionally safe.

We deliberately do **not** claim the first skeleton-to-IMU transfer, nor that any single objective is universally best; our contribution is the controlled characterization of *conditional* objective utility and its downstream reliability implications.

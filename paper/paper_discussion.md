## 1. What the results mean together

The five analyses tell one coherent story that refines — and in one place inverts — the field's prevailing intuition.

**The unifying result is the five-setting map, not any single pairwise comparison (R6e).** Ordering NTU→{CZU skeleton, UTD skeleton, Xsens quats, CZU IMU orientation-only, CZU IMU dual-raw} by gap width and target-representation strength, both the *best* prior and the *actively harmful* prior change identity as the setting moves: supervision wins at the two small, same-modality gaps; the hybrid wins and pure reconstruction is the harmful one at the middle, cross-device gap; nothing beats scratch and pure supervision is the harmful one at the two large, cross-modal gaps. This "culprit swap" — mae is the negative-transfer case only at the middle setting (R2), supLP120 only at the two large-gap settings (R6b/R6c) — is not an inconsistency to explain away; it is the finding. No pretraining objective is unconditionally safe across the skeleton→wearable gap, and *which* objective is unsafe is itself gap-dependent. The analyses below (R1–R6d) are what establish and stress-test this map; we walk through them in turn.

**Objective choice is real but gap-gated (R1).** The 45 pp within-domain spread collapsing to ~4 pp at k=1 across the modality gap is not evidence that objectives are interchangeable; it is evidence that a large representation gap acts as a low-pass filter on whatever structure the source objective built. This reconciles the two camps in the literature: the "objectives matter" results come from small-gap or within-modality settings, and the "just use a strong SSL objective" results come from settings where the gap has already been narrowed by scale or modality proximity.

**The negative-transfer case inverts "reconstruction is safe" — at the middle-sized gap (R2).** The field has drifted from supervised to reconstruction/hybrid objectives on the premise that generative, structure-preserving objectives are the robust default under shift. Our controlled cross-modal result complicates that premise: **pure masked reconstruction (mae) is the significant negative-transfer case at every shot count** (McNemar p<.001), while the *hybrid* is robustly best and *pure supervision* is competitive and best-calibrated. The resolution is that "richer objective" was always a proxy for "objective that preserves discriminative structure while remaining transferable." MAE alone satisfies neither in the cross-modal regime: it produces a barely-discriminative representation within domain (22.6%), and starting the fine-tune from that biased init lands in a worse basin than random. The hybrid keeps the discriminative signal *and* the reconstruction regularizer, which is why it, not pure reconstruction, is the safe choice here.

**Alignment is necessary, not sufficient (R3).** CKA cleanly orders the objectives (supervised aligns best, reconstruction barely above random) and does so monotonically with depth — a satisfying, scale-invariant mechanism the MMD-only view could not provide. But the best-aligned encoder is not the best-transferring, and the reconstruction encoder aligns better than scratch yet transfers worse. Representation similarity sets an upper bound on what *can* transfer; the objective's discriminative content decides what *does*. Stating this two-part conclusion — gap necessary, discriminative structure sufficient — is more honest and more useful than a single-metric claim.

**Encoder alignment and raw distributional gap turn out to be different quantities (R3).** When we extended CKA across all five source→target settings to test whether it tracks the paper's claimed small/middle/large gap ordering, it did not — czu_skeleton (claimed small gap) ranked as the *most* CKA-dissimilar of the four targets on the best-aligning encoder, below even the claimed-large setting. Rather than let that stand as an unresolved crack in the gap ordering, we measured the gap a second way, directly on a hand-crafted, encoder-free feature (no pretraining, no learned representation at all): squared-MMD and Frechet distance between NTU and each target. Both confirm the small<small<middle<large ordering exactly and monotonically. The two results are not in tension once read correctly: CKA measures how well a *specific trained encoder's* representation lines up across domains, which is objective-dependent by construction (that dependence is R3's main finding); raw MMD/Frechet measure how different the *datasets themselves* are before any model touches them. A metric computed inside a trained encoder inherits that encoder's objective-specific biases as a confound on any claim about the datasets, which is exactly why it failed to track gap width here. The five-setting map's gap ordering now rests on this encoder-free measurement, not narrative alone.

**The prior's value is calibration and data-efficiency, not headline accuracy (R4).** This is the most deployment-relevant reframe. Where endpoint accuracy is a wash, the prior still calibrates better (supLP120 lowest ECE at every k), integrates few shots faster (AUC-30 and convergence significant), and — most concretely — pays off most exactly when target data is scarcest: its benefit peaks at 0–1 enrolled subjects (+27 pp) and washes out by four. For a practitioner the actionable question is not "which objective wins at full data" (none, materially) but "how many users must I enroll before the cross-domain prior stops earning its keep" — and the answer, in this setting, is about four.

**Small differences compound into task consequences, and no objective is safe under every stress test (R5).** The controller shows that compressed recognition differences compound sharply into task-level ones, and that calibration quality sets the safety/throughput frontier for the objective that carries it. But a three-lock robustness protocol also surfaces a second, orthogonal failure mode: under randomized task design the reconstruction objective compounds worst on average, while under the one best-justified, fixed task design the best-calibrated objective's own rare, confident misclassifications dominate once safety-critical errors are penalized severely. This is the human–machine-systems payload: it converts a within-noise accuracy table into a decision-relevant reliability story — including the finding that calibration, while valuable, carries its own tail risk — and it does so on the *actual* posterior stream rather than a synthetic error model.

**The cross-modal signature is specific, and prior value is gap-contingent (R6/R6b/R6c/R6d, all pooled over 3 seeds).** That mae hurts on NTU→Xsens but *helps* on CZU skeleton→skeleton is a control, not a footnote: it shows the negative transfer is a property of the *modality gap*, not of the mae objective in the abstract, exactly as the mechanism (R3) predicts. Four independently pooled legs across three public datasets sharpen this into a monotone arc. Same-modality, the pure-supervised prior supLP120 is best-in-class and clearly significant: **+5.0 pp zero-shot on CZU (p=.001)**, independently replicated on a second dataset, **+5.0 pp on UTD-MHAD (p=.0014)** — two datasets, not one, agree that supervision wins when the gap is small (and a fifth objective, supcon, wins by an even larger margin on both). Across the modality gap with an impoverished orientation-only target (R6b), the ordering trends the opposite direction: the *prior-vs-prior* ordering trends toward **supMAE > supLP120 (29/43 folds, p=.032)**, with supLP120 trending below scratch at every k though not significantly at this sample size. The honest R6b claim is a direction — supervised-specific negative transfer partly offset by reconstruction — not a standalone significant inversion. And once the target is given its full raw signal (R6c dual-branch, where from-scratch already matches CRC), the picture sharpens rather than reverses: supMAE ties scratch (18/37, p=1.0, no detectable benefit) and supLP120 is significantly worst (10/41, **p=.0015**, paired-t significant at k=0), with supcon worse still (3/42, p<.0001, the single worst pooled result in the paper on this target). The *same* prior thus moves hero (two datasets, small gap) → villain (large gap, weak target) → clear villain (large gap, strong target), and the R6b reconstruction trend firms up into R6c's significant finding once the target is strong rather than weak. A transferred prior earns its keep only in proportion to how impoverished the target representation is — which is the negative-transfer thesis stated as a design rule, now demonstrated on three independent public datasets rather than asserted on one.

## 2. Relation to prior work

Our within-IMU reference points (BenchHAR, PRIMUS) find hybrid recon+contrastive best under OOD shift; we corroborate "hybrid is robust" in the harder *cross-modal* skeleton→IMU setting, but add the sharper finding that *pure* reconstruction can be actively harmful there — a distinction those within-modality studies could not surface because their gap is small. Against the supervised-transfer line (Moya Rueda & Fink, Awasthi), which reports supervised gains under moderate gaps, we neither confirm a supervised win nor the assumed supervised *collapse*: supervision is gap-gated to near-neutral on accuracy but retains the best calibration and the strongest low-enrollment benefit. Against the foundation/contrastive line (IMU2CLIP, UniMTS, AURA-MFM), we do not compete on scale; our contrastive arm (SupCon), run as a fifth objective across every setting in this paper, tracks the pure-supervised objective's direction closely — winning at small gap, losing at large gap — supporting the reading that label-supervision-without-reconstruction is the operative class, not one specific recipe; we are explicit that controlled small-N objective isolation is a different question than foundation-scale alignment.

## 3. Limitations (owned)

- **Five subjects, one primary dataset pair.** We address power with 3-seed pooling (n=15) and clip-level McNemar (~400–500 paired clips/subject) rather than the n=5 Wilcoxon floor (p≥0.0625), and we frame the work as a controlled characterization, not a benchmark. External validity is discharged across four pooled, multi-seed legs on three public datasets — same-modality CZU (R6, n=15) and UTD (R6d, n=24), cross-modal orientation-only CZU (R6b, ~44 folds), and cross-modal dual-branch CZU (R6c, 45 folds) — so the cross-modal effects rest on well-powered per-fold sign tests, not the single-seed n=5 floor. Two pieces remain single-seed: the R6c raw-only/quat-only diagnostic rows (the pooled *dual* mode is what carries the claim) and the CZU-dual cold-start subject-scaling sweep (a 3-seed extension is in progress). The R6c and R6b supMAE≈scratch results are nulls at current power (no *detectable* benefit, not proven equivalence).
- **The controller's magnitudes are cost-model-dependent, and so, it turns out, is its ordering claim.** Absolute task-success numbers depend on the System Input assignment, the error-cost model, and the operating threshold. Rather than freeze these, we probe them via three robustness locks (Results R5): 120 random System Input assignments, a 6-point critical-cost sweep under a fixed reliability-ranked assignment, and a tuning-free iso-safety operating point. The result is a genuine, explainable disagreement rather than a single invariant claim: under randomized assignment the reconstruction objective (mae) compounds worst; under the fixed, deployment-realistic assignment, the best-calibrated objective's own rare confident misclassifications dominate once safety-critical penalties are severe. We present this as the finding — no pretraining objective is unconditionally safe under every stress test — rather than force a single "worst objective" claim the data does not support. Inter-command timing is synthesized, not measured.
- **Subject-scaling is single-seed.** The A2 k=3 trend is robust; the k=1, N=1 spike sits in the seed-sensitive k=1 band and should be read as suggestive.
- **The swing preprocessing is a methodological finding, not an experimental lever.** We report it as evidence that surface preprocessing and feature-distance metrics are necessary-not-sufficient, having falsified its original gap-closing hypothesis; it is not part of any headline claim.

## 4. Practical guidance

For a practitioner building a wearable gesture recognizer with a skeleton prior: (i) prefer a **hybrid reconstruction+supervision** objective — pure reconstruction can hurt across a modality gap; (ii) expect the prior's accuracy benefit to be **small at full data but large at 0–1 enrolled users**, and to manifest as **better calibration and faster calibration convergence** even when endpoint accuracy is a wash; (iii) if the downstream task is a **safety-sensitive controller**, calibration quality matters for the safety/throughput trade, but is not the whole story — the best-calibrated objective can still carry a rare, confident-misfire tail risk once safety-critical roles are fixed and errors are penalized severely, so test under a realistic fixed task design, not only an averaged one; and (iv) **weigh the prior against the target's own signal** — R6c shows that once the target model can exploit its full raw stream (matching a hand-crafted baseline from scratch), the skeleton prior adds no accuracy and a pure-supervised or pure-contrastive prior can subtract it, so the prior is worth most exactly where the target representation is impoverished.
